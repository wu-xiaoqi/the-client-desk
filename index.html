Why this exists

A fixed price without a written scope is where most freelance projects go wrong — not because clients are difficult, but because "what's included" was never written down. And when an invoice slips, most freelancers either stay silent for weeks or send one blunt message that damages the relationship.

Late payment is the norm, not the exception. Remote's Contractor Management Report 2025 found 85% of freelancers have invoices paid late at least some of the time, and 21% are paid late — or not at all — more than half the time. An analysis of over 100,000 freelance invoices by Bonsai found 29% are paid at least one day late.

Sources: Remote, Contractor Management Report 2025; Bonsai invoice analysis as reported by MediaPost (secondary reporting, attributed — not independently re-run).

Structure doesn't fix a bad payer. It removes most of the avoidable cases: a written scope before you quote, a clear due date and payment link on the invoice, and a staged follow-up you don't have to invent each time.

Answers, written the way freelancers actually ask

Short, specific answers to the questions people type into Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity. No hype, no invented statistics.

What should I put in a scope of work before I quote a fixed price?

Deliverables with formats, timeline with milestones, the number of revision rounds included, explicit exclusions, and payment terms. Ask the client to reply "approved" in writing before you start.

My invoice is 30 days overdue — what should I actually send?

Escalate in stages: friendly nudge, polite reminder, clear restatement, firm note, final notice, work-pause notice at day 30, then a final request. State the invoice number, amount and days overdue; name the paused project; give the exact condition to resume; include payment details. Keep it in one thread.

How do I remind a client about an unpaid invoice without sounding rude?

Assume it slipped. State the invoice and due date plainly, remove pressure if payment is already in motion, and offer to help if something is blocking it.

What should I put on my invoice so I get paid faster?

An explicit due date rather than "on receipt", a clickable payment link, an unambiguous amount and currency, the client's PO or reference if required, and your agreed late terms in one neutral line. Put the amount and due date in the subject line too.
