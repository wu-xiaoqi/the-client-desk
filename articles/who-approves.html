How to confirm who approves deliverables before you start

More rework comes from unclear approval than from unclear instructions. Ask this in the first reply, before any work begins.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Ask who approves each deliverable in the first reply, before any work starts. One approver avoids the "my colleague wanted something else" loop; if there are two, agree in writing who has the final say.

Ask before any work starts

It takes one line in the first reply and it's much harder to introduce after the first draft has been sent around internally. Put the answer into the scope note so it's part of the agreed starting point.

Two approvers: agree who has final say

Two reviewers are fine. Two people with equal and unranked authority is not. Ask which of them signs off, and write it down. Otherwise you'll reconcile two contradictory sets of feedback on your own time.

What changes when the approver changes

If a new stakeholder appears mid-project, treat it like a scope change: confirm in writing who now approves, and whether the timeline or rounds need adjusting. Don't silently absorb the extra coordination.

Why this is a payment issue too

"It wasn't approved yet" is a common reason an invoice sits in accounts. Naming the approver and getting written sign-off up front removes that objection before it appears.

One-line version

Just to confirm before I start — who will be approving each deliverable, so I send drafts to the right person?
