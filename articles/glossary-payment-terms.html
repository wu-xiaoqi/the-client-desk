Freelance payment terms: a plain-English glossary

Most confusion on invoices comes from a few phrases everyone uses and nobody defines. Here they are, in plain English.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

An explicit calendar due date is clearer than 'due on receipt'; 'net 30' means 30 days from the invoice date; a deposit is paid before work starts; a retainer reserves ongoing availability. These are plain-English definitions and common practice, not legal definitions or advice.

The terms, defined

TermPlain-English meaningWhy it matters to you
Due on receiptPayable immediately on receiving the invoiceVague — no fixed date. Prefer an explicit date so a reminder has something to point at.
Net 7 / 14 / 30Payment due that many days after the invoice dateConfirm when the clock starts; a late PO can quietly eat into the window.
DepositA share paid before work beginsReduces how much is at risk on a new client. Commonly 30–50% in practice.
MilestoneA named stage that triggers its own invoiceSpreads payment across a long project instead of one final balance.
RetainerA recurring fee that reserves ongoing availabilityPaid for access to your time, usually monthly, not for a single deliverable.
PO / purchase orderThe client's internal reference authorising the purchaseMissing POs are a common reason invoices stall in accounts payable.

Why the due date wording matters

"Due on receipt" sets no visible clock. An explicit date — "due 14 November 2026" — gives both sides the same reference point and makes a later follow-up a factual statement rather than a complaint.

Put the terms in the scope note

Terms that live only in a separate email are the ones nobody can find later. Keep the split, the due-date rule and any PO requirement in the same written document as the deliverables.

Where the plain English stops

These are working definitions and common practice, not legal definitions and not legal advice. Some jurisdictions have specific rules about freelance contracts and payment timing. For an actual contract or dispute, consult a qualified professional in your jurisdiction.

One neutral terms line for your scope note

Payment: [50%] to start, [50%] on delivery; each invoice due within [14] days of issue. Please add PO [number] so accounts can process it without a query.
