Fixed price or hourly rate? How to choose

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on how well the scope can be written down before work starts.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Quote a fixed price when deliverables and revision rounds can be written down precisely; use hourly when the scope is genuinely open-ended. If a client wants a fixed number on a vague brief, fix the scope first, not the price.

At a glance

Fixed priceHourly rate
Best whenDeliverables, formats and round counts are clearScope is exploratory or likely to change
Client seesOne number, known up frontA rate, and a total that grows
Your riskUnpaid extra work if scope wasn't written downFewer bookings from clients who need certainty
NeedsA written scope and explicit exclusionsA cap or estimate, and a time log

Choose fixed price when

  • You can list the exact deliverables and file formats.
  • You can state how many revision rounds are included.
  • You can name what is not included, to be quoted separately.
  • The client needs a number to get approval internally.

Choose hourly when

  • The work is research, discovery or ongoing support with no fixed end.
  • The client keeps adding stakeholders and directions.
  • You cannot reasonably estimate the volume yet.

Even then, give a written estimate and a cap so the client can budget. "Hourly, estimated [n] hours at $[rate], I'll flag before exceeding [cap]" removes most of the friction.

The trap to avoid

A fixed price on a vague scope is the worst of both: the client expects certainty, and you absorb every change. If the brief is thin, send the scope note first and quote after it is confirmed. Asking for clarity is not losing the job.

Proposing a fixed price on a thin brief

Hi [Name], happy to put a fixed price together. Before I do, could you confirm the deliverables and formats, the deadline, and how many revision rounds are included? Once that's written down I'll send a fixed fee with clear exclusions, so there are no surprises on either side. [You]
