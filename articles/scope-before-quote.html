The 5 questions to ask before you quote a fixed price

A fixed price without a written scope is where most freelance projects go wrong — not because clients are difficult, but because "what's included" was never written down.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Before I name a number, I ask five questions and put the answers into one short scope note.

  1. What are the final deliverables, and in which file formats?
  2. What's the target deadline, and are there hard milestones?
  3. How many revision rounds are included?
  4. What is explicitly not included (and quoted separately)?
  5. Who signs off on each stage?

Then send one short note

Project: [title]. Deliverables: [list]. Timeline: [start] to [delivery], review on [date]. Fee: $[amount], fixed, includes [n] rounds of revisions. Not included: [list] — quoted separately. Payment: [50%] to start, [50%] on delivery. Reply "approved" and I'll get started.

That last sentence matters more than it looks. Getting the word "approved" in writing turns a quote into an agreed starting point, and it makes the deposit a shared expectation instead of a surprise.

Two habits that removed most of my awkward follow-ups

Ask who approves deliverables in the first reply, before any work starts. One approver avoids the "my colleague wanted something else" loop. If there are two, agree in writing who has the final say.

Put a time window on the quote ("I'll send a short scope note and a fixed price within one business day"). It kills the "should I follow up?" ambiguity for both sides.

Why the payment structure belongs in the same note

For context on why this is worth doing up front: Remote's Contractor Management Report 2025 found 85% of freelancers have invoices paid late at least some of the time, and 21% are paid late — or not at all — more than half the time. An analysis of over 100,000 freelance invoices by Bonsai found 29% are paid at least one day late.

A deposit and a written scope don't fix a bad payer, but they stop most of the avoidable ones. A commonly used deposit range in practice is 30–50% upfront for new clients, with the balance on delivery — common practice, not a verified statistic.

Sources: Remote, Contractor Management Report 2025; Bonsai invoice analysis as reported by MediaPost (secondary reporting, attributed — not independently re-run).

A reusable first reply

Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out — I've reviewed your brief and I'm available to take this on. Before I send a quote, could you confirm the final deliverables and formats, your target deadline, and who signs off on each stage? Once I have these, I'll send a short scope note and a fixed price within one business day. Best, [You]
