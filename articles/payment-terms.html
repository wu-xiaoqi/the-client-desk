How to set payment terms a client will actually follow

Payment terms aren't just accounting — they're the first instruction your client's accounts team follows. Make it unambiguous.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Set payment terms that match how the client actually pays: 'due on receipt' for small or new clients, or a stated net term (commonly net 14–30) where the client's process needs it. State the due date as a concrete date, not just 'net 30', and put the payment link on the invoice itself. Clear terms are what make a later reminder a factual nudge, not a confrontation.

Pick the term, then name the date

'Net 30' is a window; 'due 2026-12-15' is an instruction. Clients who pay on a cycle act on the date. Always translate the term into a real due date on the invoice.

Match the term to the relationship

  • New or small client → due on receipt, or a deposit up front.
  • Established client with a billing cycle → a stated net term they confirm.
  • Large first project → deposit regardless of term.

The right term reduces friction; the wrong one just delays the conversation.

Put the link where the money is

An invoice with the payment link embedded gets paid faster than one that sends the client hunting for where to pay. Stating terms and the link together removes the two most common reasons for a late payment.

Consistency is the real lever

The terms you state on invoice one are the terms invoice five will be judged against. Match them to the scope note so every reminder references the same agreed rule.

Reusable terms line

Payment terms: due on receipt (or Net [30] — due [date]). Pay here: [payment link]. These match the terms in our scope note so there are no surprises at either end. [You]
