Should you ask a new client for a deposit?

For new clients with no payment history, a deposit is the single most effective thing you can do before any work begins.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

For new clients, a deposit removes most collection problems before they start. A commonly used range in practice is 30–50% upfront with the balance on delivery — written into the scope note before work begins. This is common practice, not a verified statistic.

Why a deposit helps

It secures a place in your schedule, covers initial research and setup, and — most importantly — turns payment into a shared expectation rather than a surprise at the end. It also means a problem shows up early, while the amount at risk is still small.

A commonly used range

A commonly used range in practice is 25–50% upfront, with 30–50% typical for new clients and closer to 50% when there's no payment history. This is common practice, not a measured statistic — we're not going to invent a number and call it data.

Write it into the scope note

Put the split in the same note as the deliverables and timeline, before anyone starts. If the client pushes back, the conversation is about a concrete number and a rationale, not about whether they trust you.

A yellow flag worth noticing

A new client who wants all payment deferred until the very end is asking you to absorb the entire risk. That's worth a conversation before you begin. For an established client with a clean history, the calculus is different.

One-line scope-note version

Payment: [50%] to start, [50%] on delivery. Reply "approved" and I'll get started.
