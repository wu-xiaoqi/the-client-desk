How to send your first invoice to a new client

The first invoice to a new client establishes the rhythm for every invoice after it. Get it right once and the rest is repetition.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Before the first invoice, confirm the client's invoicing requirements (PO, reference, billing contact) in writing. Send the invoice immediately on completion, with an explicit due date and a clickable payment link in both the invoice and the email subject line. Match the terms you agreed in the scope note — consistency is what makes later reminders factual.

Confirm invoicing requirements first

Before you start, ask whether the client needs a PO number, a specific reference, or a named billing contact. Missing these is the most common reason a first invoice stalls in accounts payable — and it's far easier to ask in the scope note than to chase after.

Send it the moment work completes

An invoice sent immediately on delivery starts the clock the client can see. Delaying it by a week doesn't read as polite; it just moves the due date later and makes your own follow-up feel late.

Make the subject line do the work

"Invoice [number] — $[amount] — due [date]" lets an accounts team act without opening the email. It also makes any later reminder a reference to a date both sides can see, not a complaint.

Match the agreed terms

The first invoice should reflect exactly the split, due-date rule and reference you wrote in the scope note. Consistency here is what lets you follow up later without re-litigating the terms.

Reusable first-invoice email

Subject: Invoice [number] — $[amount] — due [date]

Hi [Name], the work on [project] is complete and handed over. Invoice [number] for $[amount], due [date], is attached. Please add PO [reference] if your process requires it, and you can pay here: [payment link]. Thanks, [You]
