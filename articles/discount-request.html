How to respond when a client asks for a discount

A discount request is usually a negotiation opener, not a final demand. The goal is to hold your price without making it a confrontation.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Respond to a discount request by separating price from scope: offer a smaller scoped option or a payment-structure change instead of a lower rate. If you do discount, tie it to something concrete — a longer commitment, a faster decision, or a bundled deliverable — so the discount has a reason and a limit.

Don't answer the discount directly

The instinct is to say yes or no to the number. Better to step back and ask what's driving it — budget, comparison, or a test of your confidence. The answer changes what you offer. A request rooted in a real budget cap is different from one that's a reflex.

Trade scope, not rate

  • Offer a smaller scoped version at your normal rate.
  • Offer a phased start so the client commits less up front.
  • Offer a payment-structure change (deposit + milestone) instead of a lower total.

These keep your rate intact while still giving the client a way to say yes within their constraint.

If you do discount, tie it to something

A discount with no reason becomes the new baseline. If you reduce the fee, attach it to a concrete trigger — a longer contract, a faster decision, or a bundled add-on — and say so in writing. That keeps the discount a business decision, not a precedent.

Keep the relationship intact

"I can't move the rate, but here's how we can make the number work" holds the line without making it personal. The client remembers whether the conversation felt collaborative, not whether they got 10% off.

Reusable discount-response template

Hi [Name], I hear you on the budget — happy to find a way to make this work. Rather than change the rate, I can either scope a smaller first phase at the same rate, or split payment into [deposit + milestone] so the up-front number is lower. If we lock a [longer engagement / faster decision] I can include [specific add-on] at no extra cost. Which direction works better for you? [You]
