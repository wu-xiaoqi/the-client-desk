How to reply to a first client inquiry (with template)

A short, specific reply beats a long pitch. The goal of the first message isn't to win the whole project — it's to get the information you need to quote accurately.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

A reliable first reply has four parts: acknowledge, confirm availability, ask three qualifying questions, and state the next step with a time window.

The four parts

  1. Acknowledge the brief and confirm you're available.
  2. Ask three qualifying questions before you quote.
  3. State the next step.
  4. Give a time window — for example, one business day.

The three questions worth asking

  • What are the final deliverables, and in which file formats?
  • What's the target deadline, and are there hard milestones?
  • Who signs off on each stage?

These three remove most of the ambiguity that turns into scope creep later. The third one is the most often skipped — and the most expensive to skip.

Why the time window matters

Saying "I'll send a short scope note and a fixed price within one business day" sets expectations on both sides and removes the "should I follow up?" ambiguity. It also stops you from quoting before you have the answers.

What to avoid

  • A long pitch listing everything you've ever done.
  • Naming a fixed price before the scope is written down.
  • Saying yes to every request in the first message — it becomes the assumed scope.

Reusable first-reply template

Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out — I've reviewed your brief and I'm available to take this on. Before I send a quote, could you confirm the final deliverables and formats, your target deadline, and who signs off on each stage? Once I have these, I'll send a short scope note and a fixed price within one business day. Best, [You]
