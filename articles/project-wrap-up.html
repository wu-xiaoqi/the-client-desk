How to close a project and ask for a testimonial

The end of a project is the moment the value is freshest — and the moment most freelancers rush through with a one-line "here are the files".

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Close on three things — delivery, handover, and a soft ask. Ask for the testimonial when the value is freshest, and offer to draft it so saying yes takes a minute.

Close on three things

  1. Delivery — confirm everything agreed is delivered, and where the final files live.
  2. Handover — a short note on how to use or maintain what you built.
  3. A soft ask — one question, no pressure.

Ask while the value is fresh

A testimonial requested two days after a smooth handover is easy to write. The same request three months later feels like a favour. Timing matters more than wording.

Make it take a minute

Offering to draft two lines for the client to edit removes the blank-page problem. Most people are happy to approve or tweak something; far fewer will write from scratch. Let them edit it so the words stay genuinely theirs.

Keep the door open

A close-out is also the natural moment to mention you're available for the next project. One sentence is enough — don't turn the handover email into a pitch.

Reusable wrap-up template

Hi [Name], everything is delivered and handed over — final files are at [link], and I've included a short handover note. It was a pleasure working with you. If you're happy with the result, would you be open to a two-line testimonial I can quote? I can draft something for you to edit so it takes a minute. Thanks again, [You]
