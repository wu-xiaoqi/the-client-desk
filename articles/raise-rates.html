How to raise your rates with existing clients

Existing clients are the easiest people to keep and the hardest to reprice. The trick is to give notice, not to apologise.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Raise rates for existing clients with clear written notice (commonly 30–60 days), apply the new rate only to new work rather than re-pricing what's already agreed, and frame it as a normal business update. Most steady clients accept a stated, scheduled increase far better than an unannounced one.

Give notice, not an apology

A rate change is a business update, not a favour you're asking. State the new rate and the date it takes effect. A common practice is to give 30–60 days' notice so the client can plan — this is general practice, not a legal requirement.

Apply it to new work only

Don't re-price work already agreed or in progress. Apply the new rate to new projects or new phases from the effective date. This removes the feeling of a moving target and keeps the trust you've built.

Make the increase easy to accept

  • State the new rate plainly.
  • Name the effective date.
  • Confirm it applies to new work from that date.
  • Offer to lock current pricing on any project booked before the date.

What if they push back

A steady client who balks may simply need the notice period. One who leaves over a scheduled, stated increase was likely always going to be price-sensitive. Either outcome is information, not a crisis.

Reusable rate-change notice

Hi [Name], a quick heads-up on my rates: from [date, 30–60 days out] my rate moves to $[new rate]. This applies to new work from that date; anything already agreed stays at the current rate. If you'd like to book [project] before then at the current rate, just say the word. Thanks for the work together, [You]
