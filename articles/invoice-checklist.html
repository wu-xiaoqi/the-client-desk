The invoice checklist that gets you paid faster

Most "late" payments trace back to a vague invoice, not a difficult client. Fix the invoice and you chase far less.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Put an explicit due date (not "on receipt"), a clickable payment link, an unambiguous amount and currency, the client's PO or reference if required, and your agreed late terms in one neutral line. Put the amount and due date in the email subject line too.

Five fields that remove most delays

FieldWhat to writeWhy it helps
Due dateAn explicit date, not "on receipt"Sets the clock everyone can see
Payment linkA clickable method, right in the invoiceRemoves "how do I pay?"
Amount & currencyUnambiguous totalAvoids approval delays
PO / referenceTheir internal reference if requiredClears accounts-payable queues
Late termsYour agreed terms, in one neutral lineMakes later reminders factual

Put it in the subject line

Accounts teams triage by subject line. "Invoice [number] — $[amount] — due [date]" lets them act without opening the email. It also makes your own follow-up factual: you're referring to a date both sides can see.

Send it the moment work is done

Waiting a week to invoice doesn't make you look polite; it just moves the clock. Digital invoicing sent immediately on completion is consistently associated with faster payment than paper or delayed processes. [General practice; no specific statistic claimed here.]

Then stop improvising

Once the invoice is right, follow up on a schedule rather than when you feel annoyed. A staged ladder keeps the relationship intact and gives you a clean written record.

Reusable invoice email

Subject: Invoice [number] — $[amount] — due [date]

Hi [Name], the work on [project] is complete and handed over. Invoice [number] for $[amount] is attached, due [date]. You can pay here: [payment link]. If your accounts team needs a PO or reference, it's [reference]. Thanks, [You]
