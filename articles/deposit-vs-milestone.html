Deposit or milestone billing? How to choose

Both reduce how much of your work is unpaid at any one moment. They solve the problem at different project sizes.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

A single deposit suits small, short projects with a new client. Milestone splits suit larger or longer projects. The principle behind both is the same: don't let the entire fee become one invoice due only at the very end.

At a glance

Upfront depositMilestone billing
Best whenSmall / short project, new clientLarger or multi-week project
Structure[30–50%] to start, balance on deliverySplit against named stages, invoiced as each completes
Protects againstDoing all the work before any paymentA large balance sitting unpaid at the end
AdminOne split, two invoicesMore invoices, one per milestone

Choose a deposit when

  • The project is short enough that one start payment covers your initial risk.
  • The client is new and has no payment history with you.
  • You want to book the slot and cover setup work.

A commonly used range in practice is 30–50% upfront for new clients — common practice, not a verified statistic.

Choose milestones when

  • The project spans several weeks or has natural stages.
  • The final amount would be a large single balance.
  • You want each completed stage paid before starting the next.

Name the stages and dates in the same written scope note as the deliverables, so each invoice refers to something both sides already agreed.

You can combine them

A kickoff deposit followed by milestone invoices is common on larger projects. The point is not which label you use — it is that payment is spread across the work rather than all deferred to the end.

Proposing a split

Hi [Name], for a project this size I'd suggest [payment structure: 50% to start and 50% on delivery / invoiced at kickoff, first draft and final delivery], each invoice due within [7] days. That keeps both sides' exposure small. Does that work for your process? I'll put the agreed version in the scope note. [You]
