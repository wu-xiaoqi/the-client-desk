How to run a project kickoff with a new client

The kickoff is where alignment is cheapest. Get it in writing once and you reference it every time something drifts.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

Run a kickoff that confirms the goal, the deliverables, the decision-maker, the timeline, and the payment structure — all in one written summary the client confirms. A five-point kickoff note is what you point back to when scope, approver, or terms get questioned later.

Confirm the goal in their words

Restate what success looks like from the client's view and ask them to confirm. A one-line agreement on the goal ends most 'that's not what I meant' disputes before they start.

Pin the deliverables and the approver

List the deliverables and name who approves them. Linking the approver to who-approves discipline early stops the 'my manager wants changes' surprise at the end.

State the timeline and payment in the same note

  • Milestone dates tied to deliverables.
  • Deposit taken, or milestone split agreed.
  • Invoice timing stated plainly.

Keeping schedule and money in the kickoff note means later reminders reference something the client already accepted.

Send it as a confirmation, not a question

'Here's what we agreed — reply to confirm' reads as professional and gives the client one easy action. It also becomes your written baseline.

Reusable kickoff summary

Hi [Name], to confirm our kickoff: Goal — [their words]. Deliverables — [list], approved by [name]. Timeline — [milestones]. Payment — [deposit / milestone split], invoice on [timing]. Reply to confirm and I'll begin [date]. [You]
