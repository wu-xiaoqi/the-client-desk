What to do when a client goes quiet after the kickoff

Silence after kickoff usually means their priority shifted, not that they're unhappy. The response should reopen the loop, not accuse.

Published 1 November 2026 · The Client Desk · communication templates, not legal advice

Short answer

When a client goes quiet after kickoff, send one calm check-in that assumes good faith, states where you are, and offers a clear next step with a date. If it's a new client with a deposit, the exposure is smaller; if no deposit was taken, treat the silence as a reason to confirm payment terms before resuming.

Assume good faith first

Most silence is a shifted priority, a stalled internal approval, or a busy week — not dissatisfaction. The first message should reopen the conversation, not demand an explanation. A neutral check-in keeps the door open.

Make the check-in easy to answer

  • State exactly where the work is.
  • Name the one decision you need.
  • Offer a date by which a reply would keep the timeline intact.

A question with a clear answer gets answered; an open "just checking in" often doesn't.

Tie it to the commercial line

If you took a deposit, your exposure is bounded and a gentle pause is reasonable. If no deposit was taken, the silence is a signal to confirm payment structure before doing more. That's a business point, not a personal one.

Know when to stop

After two calm, dated attempts with no reply, it's reasonable to pause and let the client re-engage. Document the attempts in one thread — it's your record if the project later resurfaces or if a fee discussion follows.

Reusable kickoff check-in

Hi [Name], just circling back — I've completed [the kickoff / phase 1] and am ready for the next step. To keep the timeline on track, could you confirm [the one decision needed] by [date]? If priorities have shifted on your side, no problem — just let me know and we'll adjust. [You]
